## Background

The WIPO Diplomatic Conference concluded on **May 24, 2024**, adopting the **Treaty on Intellectual Property, Genetic Resources and Associated Traditional Knowledge (GRATK Treaty)**. India actively participated in and supported this treaty.

## Article 3 – Disclosure Requirement

**Core obligation**: Contracting Parties shall require patent applicants to disclose:

1. **Country of origin** of the genetic resource that the invention is based on or derived from
2. **Source of the traditional knowledge** associated with genetic resources, where the invention directly utilizes traditional knowledge

For Ayurveda: This means that a patent application for a drug derived from an Indian herb (genetic resource) must disclose India as the country of origin.

## Article 4 – Flexibilities

- Contracting parties may implement disclosure obligations through existing frameworks
- Disclosure of "country of origin" vs. "source" (more flexible)
- Sanctions for non-disclosure: Parties may apply sanctions including invalidation, rejection, or administrative sanctions

## Article 5 – Database Mechanism

Parties shall ensure patent examiners have access to minimum documentation including traditional knowledge databases (like TKDL) for prior art examination.

## Implications for Ayurveda Patent Applications

| Scenario | Disclosure Required |
|---|---|
| Drug from Indian herb (even synthetic analog) | Country of origin = India |
| Formulation based on TKDL-documented knowledge | Traditional knowledge source must be disclosed |
| Novel delivery system (no TK link) | No disclosure required |
| International PCT application | Disclose in requesting office |

## Status

- Adopted: May 2024, Geneva
- Open for signature: July 2024
- Entry into force: After 15 instruments of ratification
- India's status: Active negotiator; ratification expected
