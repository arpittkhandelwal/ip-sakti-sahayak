## Overview

The **Nagoya Protocol on Access to Genetic Resources and the Fair and Equitable Sharing of Benefits Arising from their Utilization** is a supplementary agreement to the **Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)**. Adopted on October 29, 2010, in Nagoya, Japan. India ratified in 2012.

## Article 6 – Access to Genetic Resources

Each Party shall take measures to ensure that:
1. **Prior Informed Consent (PIC)** of the provider country is required before accessing genetic resources
2. **Mutually Agreed Terms (MAT)** are established between provider and user
3. Access permits or equivalent documentation are issued

For Ayurveda: Indian herbs and medicinal plants are genetic resources. Exporting them or using their derivatives for commercial development abroad requires PIC from India (via NBA) and MAT.

## Article 7 – Access to Traditional Knowledge

Where traditional knowledge associated with genetic resources is accessed, Parties shall take measures to ensure PIC or approval and involvement of **indigenous and local communities**.

This directly applies to Ayurvedic traditional knowledge – pharmaceutical companies seeking to use documented Ayurvedic formulas for drug development internationally must obtain consent from Indian authorities and potentially from knowledge-holding communities.

## Article 17 – ABS Clearing-House

**IRCC (Internationally Recognized Certificate of Compliance)**: When PIC is granted and MAT established, an IRCC is issued and published in the **ABS Clearing-House** (https://absch.cbd.int) as proof of legal access.

## Practical Steps for Ayurvedic Exporters

1. **Identify** genetic resources or TK in your product
2. **Apply** to NBA India for PIC (Form I under BD Act 2002)
3. **Negotiate** MAT including monetary benefit-sharing terms
4. **Receive** IRCC from NBA, registered in ABS Clearing-House
5. **Provide** IRCC to importing country's checkpoint authorities

## EU Regulation 511/2014 – Key Checkpoint

Companies marketing products in the EU that use genetic resources must exercise **due diligence** to verify legal access and benefit-sharing compliance. Non-compliance can result in administrative sanctions.
