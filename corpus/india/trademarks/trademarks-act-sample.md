## Overview

The **Trade Marks Act, 1999** governs the registration and protection of trademarks for goods and services in India. Because classical Ayurvedic formulations cannot be patented per Section 3(p), **brand trademarks** constitute one of the primary commercial IP assets for AYUSH enterprises.

## Classification (Nice Classification)

- **Class 5**: Ayurvedic medicines, medicinal herbal preparations, pharmaceutical substances, dietary supplements for medical use
- **Class 3**: Ayurvedic cosmetics, non-medicated soaps, herbal hair oils, skincare preparations
- **Class 30**: Herbal teas, spices, non-medicinal food preparations
- **Class 44**: Ayurvedic clinical services, Panchakarma therapy centres, hospitals

## Absolute Grounds for Refusal (Section 9)

A trademark will be refused if:
1. It lacks distinctive character
2. It consists exclusively of marks or indications designating the kind, quality, intended purpose, or geographical origin of goods (e.g., trying to trademark "Pure Ashwagandha" or "Triphala Churna" alone)
3. It consists exclusively of generic names of Ayurvedic herbs or classical preparations

## Strategies for Ayurveda Businesses

- Combine distinctive coined terms with descriptive herb names (e.g., "Kairali Dasamoolarishtam" rather than "Dasamoolarishtam" alone)
- Register distinctive logo/device marks and packaging trade dress
- Conduct clearance searches on IP India's online trademark search portal before product launch
