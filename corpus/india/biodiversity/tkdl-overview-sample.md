## What is TKDL?

The **Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL)** is a collaborative project between the **Ministry of AYUSH** and the **Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR)**, India. It digitizes traditional medicinal knowledge from classical Sanskrit, Urdu, Arabic, and Tamil texts into searchable, structured formats in multiple languages.

## Scope and Content

- **~3,00,000+ traditional medicinal formulations** from Ayurveda, Unani, Siddha, and Yoga
- Sourced from 900+ classical texts
- Classified using **Traditional Knowledge Resource Classification (TKRC)** – a sub-classification of IPC (International Patent Classification)
- Available in English, Hindi, German, French, Spanish, Japanese

## Role in Patent Examination

TKDL has signed **access agreements** with major patent offices:

| Patent Office | Agreement Year |
|---|---|
| European Patent Office (EPO) | 2009 |
| United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) | 2009 |
| UK Intellectual Property Office | 2010 |
| German Patent Office | 2010 |
| Japanese Patent Office | 2013 |
| Canadian IP Office | 2010 |

**Access for Examination Only**: TKDL data is available to patent examiners for prior art searches but NOT for public access or commercial use without TKDL's authorization.

## Success Stories

- **Turmeric (Curcuma longa)**: USPTO revoked patent on turmeric's wound-healing properties (US5401504) after TKDL evidence
- **Neem**: EPO revoked fungicidal neem preparation patent after India challenged it using traditional knowledge documents
- **Phyllanthus (Bhumyamalaki)**: Multiple hepatitis treatment patent challenges successful

## How to Search TKDL

1. Visit https://tkdl.res.in (public access to index only; full text via IP office agreements)
2. AYUSH practitioners and researchers: apply for TKDL access via CSIR
3. Patent applicants (India): Submit TKDL search results with patent application Form 1

## Relationship to Section 3(p)

Any formulation, plant use, or therapeutic application documented in TKDL constitutes prior art for Section 3(p) purposes. If your invention is covered by a TKDL entry, it is non-patentable in India.
