## Classification of Ayurvedic Drugs

### Classical Drugs (First Schedule)

**Definition**: Drugs described in the authoritative books of Ayurvedic, Siddha, and Unani systems listed in the **First Schedule** to the Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940.

Authoritative texts include:
- Ayurveda: Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita, Ashtanga Hridayam, Bhavaprakasha Nighantu, Sarangadhara Samhita
- Reference works: API (Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India) Volumes I–XI

**Regulatory pathway**: 
- Must be manufactured per classical text formulations
- Requires pharmacopoeial compliance (API standards)
- Schedule E drugs list those requiring caution labels
- No clinical trial requirement if already in classical text

**IP Posture**: Classical drugs are NON-PATENTABLE per Section 3(p) of the Patents Act. Brand names (trademarks) and GI tags (if region-specific) may be available.

### Patent/Proprietary Drugs

**Definition**: Drugs not described in any authoritative book but having composition of Ayurvedic ingredients proprietary to the manufacturer.

**Regulatory pathway**:
- Requires CDSCO/State Drugs Controller approval
- Must file Form 44 with composition details
- Safety and efficacy data may be required
- Must not make therapeutic claims without approval

**IP Posture**: Patent protection possible if novel and not based solely on traditional knowledge. Trademark protection available for brand names.

## Schedule Y-A – Phytopharmaceuticals

Drugs derived from plants through defined extraction processes, different from classical Ayurvedic drugs:
- Required: Ayurvedic pharmacopoeial monograph OR safety data
- Phase I-III clinical trial may be required
- Separate from AYUSH drugs – closer to conventional new drug approval

## ABS Consideration

Both classical and proprietary Ayurvedic drugs using biological resources from India must comply with the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (as amended 2023) – prior intimation to SBB (domestic companies) or NBA approval (foreign-linked entities).
