## Section 3(p) – What is Not Patentable

The following are not inventions within the meaning of this Act—

> **(p)** an invention which, in effect, is traditional knowledge or which is an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component or components.

**Explanation**: This provision explicitly bars patents on inventions that are based on or constitute traditional knowledge. Any formulation, preparation, or therapeutic use documented in classical Ayurvedic, Unani, Siddha, or Yoga texts is considered traditional knowledge and falls within this exclusion.

## Key Implications for Ayurveda

- **Classical formulations** from texts like Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita, Ashtanga Hridayam are non-patentable per se
- **Known herbs used in known ways** are excluded from patent protection
- **Novel dosage forms** or **novel delivery mechanisms** (e.g., nanoparticle encapsulation) may be patentable if truly inventive and meeting the full criteria of novelty, inventive step, and industrial applicability
- **Novel extraction processes** (not based on traditional methods) may be patentable as process claims
- The **TKDL** (Traditional Knowledge Digital Library) serves as an authoritative prior art database to identify Section 3(p) exclusions

## Related Provisions

- Section 2(1)(l): Definition of "new invention" – must not be anticipated by prior publication
- Section 3(d): Enhanced efficacy requirement (also relevant for Ayurvedic new drugs)
- Section 3(e): Mixture of known substances unless synergy demonstrated
- Section 25: Opposition to patent grant (can be based on Section 3(p) grounds)
- Section 64: Revocation of patent (available on Section 3(p) grounds)

## IP Posture Summary

| Product Type | Patent Eligibility | Alternative IP |
|---|---|---|
| Classical formulation | ❌ Not patentable (Section 3p) | GI, Trademark |
| Known herb, known use | ❌ Not patentable | Trade Secret (limited) |
| Novel extraction process | ✅ Possibly patentable | Trade Secret |
| Novel delivery system | ✅ Possibly patentable | Design (packaging) |
| Novel combination (with synergy proof) | ✅ Possibly patentable | — |
