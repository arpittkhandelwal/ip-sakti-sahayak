## Overview

The **GI Act, 1999** provides for registration and protection of Geographical Indications (GI) relating to goods in India. It is administered by the **Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (CGPDTM)** via the GI Registry, Chennai.

## What Can Be Registered as a GI

A geographical indication that:
1. Identifies goods as **originating from a territory**, region, or locality
2. Where **quality, reputation, or characteristic** is attributable to that geographical origin
3. Covers agricultural, natural, manufactured, or handicraft goods

## Application Process

1. **Who can apply**: Producers' associations, groups of persons, State Governments
2. **Form**: GI-1 with statement of case, class of goods, map, standards, inspection structure
3. **Filing fee**: Prescribed fee at GI Registry, IP India
4. **Examination**: GI Registrar examines for compliance
5. **Publication**: Published in GI Journal for public opposition (3 months)
6. **Registration**: Valid for 10 years, renewable indefinitely

## Protection Under GI Act

- **Section 22**: Civil remedy against unauthorized use of registered GI
- Infringement: Using GI on goods not originating from the stated region
- **Authorized users** can sue for infringement; registered proprietor has right to prevent use

## Significance for Ayurveda

- Regional Ayurvedic preparations (e.g., Kerala Panchakarma methods, specific formulation schools) may qualify for GI protection
- GI protects the collective reputation of a region's Ayurvedic tradition
- Cannot replace trademark protection for individual brand names – both may be used together

## International GI Protection

- India's GIs protected in WTO member countries under TRIPS Article 22-24
- Additional protection: Bilateral agreements with EU (ongoing negotiations)
